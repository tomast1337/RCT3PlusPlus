# Code of Conduct

rct3++ has two sets of rules: how we treat each other, and where our knowledge of the original game may
come from. Both apply to everyone who takes part: issues, pull requests, reviews, discussions, and any
other place the project is represented.

## Our pledge

We pledge to make taking part in this project a harassment-free experience for everyone, regardless of
age, body size, visible or invisible disability, ethnicity, sex characteristics, gender identity and
expression, level of experience, education, socio-economic status, nationality, personal appearance,
race, caste, colour, religion, or sexual identity and orientation.

We will act in ways that make for an open, welcoming, diverse, inclusive and healthy community.

## Our standards

Behaviour that helps:

- Being kind and patient, especially with people new to the project or to programming.
- Respecting differing opinions, viewpoints and experiences.
- Giving constructive feedback, and taking it gracefully.
- Owning our mistakes, apologising to those affected, and learning from them.
- Putting what is best for the project and its community ahead of personal preference.

Behaviour that is not acceptable:

- Sexualised language or imagery, and sexual attention or advances of any kind.
- Trolling, insults, derogatory comments, and personal or political attacks.
- Public or private harassment.
- Publishing other people's private information, such as a physical or email address, without their
  explicit permission.
- Any other conduct that could reasonably be considered inappropriate in a professional setting.

## Clean-room standards

rct3++ is a clean-room reimplementation. It must stay that way, for the project's sake and for that of
everyone who contributes to it. Breaking these rules is a conduct violation, handled like any other.

We do not accept, and we will not use:

- Code, pseudocode, structure layouts, function names or addresses obtained by decompiling,
  disassembling or debugging the original game's executables or libraries.
- Leaked, stolen or otherwise unlicensed source code, tools or documents from the original developers or
  publishers.
- Game files or anything copied out of them: assets, text, sounds, models, saves, or byte dumps.
- Contributions from anyone who cannot tell where their knowledge came from.

Do not post any of the above in issues, pull requests, discussions or comments either, not even as a
quote, a link or "just for reference".

Knowledge may come from:

- **The data files, read as data.** Opening the game's own data, save and scenario files and describing
  what is in them, in our own words, the way modding tools have read them for years so that people can
  use their own files. Format descriptions in this repository are written this way.
- **Watching the game play.** When the game behaves in a way that only the program knows (a speed, a
  rate, a threshold, a timing), we treat the exact value as unknown. We measure the behaviour in the
  running game, with experiments anyone can repeat, and approximate it. The code says that the value is
  an approximation and how it was measured.
- **Our own model.** Where measuring is not practical, we design our own behaviour and our own values.
  rct3++ does not need to match the original number for number.
- **Public, openly licensed work**, such as OpenRCT2, under a compatible licence and credited at the
  place of use (see [CLEAN_ROOM.md](CLEAN_ROOM.md)).
- **Public documentation**: the game's manual, published interviews and community knowledge written by
  players, summarised in our own words.

If you are not sure whether something is allowed, ask before you contribute it.

## Enforcement responsibilities

Maintainers clarify and enforce these standards, and take fair action in response to behaviour they
consider inappropriate, threatening, offensive or harmful, or to contributions that break the clean-room
standards.

Maintainers may remove, edit or reject comments, commits, code, issues and other contributions that do
not follow this Code of Conduct, and will explain moderation decisions when appropriate. Material that
breaks the clean-room standards is removed, including from the repository's history when needed.

## Reporting

Report unacceptable behaviour, or material you believe breaks the clean-room standards, to the maintainer
at vycasnicolas@gmail.com. Every report is reviewed promptly and fairly, and the reporter's privacy is
respected.

## Enforcement guidelines

1. **Correction.** For unprofessional or unwelcome language or behaviour, or a clean-room mistake made in
   good faith: a private, written warning explaining the problem. The material is removed. A public
   apology may be requested.
2. **Warning.** For a single serious incident or a series of them: a warning with consequences for
   continued behaviour, and no interaction with the people involved for a set time.
3. **Temporary ban.** For a serious violation, including sustained inappropriate behaviour or knowingly
   submitting material that breaks the clean-room standards: a ban from all interaction with the project
   for a set time.
4. **Permanent ban.** For a pattern of violations, harassment of an individual, aggression towards or
   disparagement of groups of people, or repeated clean-room violations: a permanent ban.
